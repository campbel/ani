Letter to shareholders

Dear shareholders, employees, trial members, and the one hunter whose pet keeps eating the inventory,

Fiscal year 2026 was a year of consolidation. We entered it with an undersized trading desk, an overleveraged banana position, and a raid calendar that, frankly, nobody had read. We exit it with thirty-one people on the desk, a fully stocked guild bank, and a reputation on the dock that we did not have twelve months ago. Harvest volumes grew double digits. Attendance stabilized. Nobody stood in the fire more than twice in the same week.

Fiscal 2027 is the year we grow into that position. The guidance in this document is ambitious. It assumes a full trading desk, a full raid calendar, and bananas that ripen on schedule. Two of those three are within our control. We are comfortable with those odds; we have raided on worse.

Our strategy has three parts. First, scale the desk: forty seats, filled with people who show up. Second, clear the calendar: every tier of content on the schedule, in the quarter it arrives. Third, protect the balance sheet: a banana reserve that lets us absorb a bad month without asking anyone to farm on a Saturday.

I want to be direct about the risks. Our business depends on a steady supply of people with free evenings, a patch cadence we do not set, and a jungle full of trolls who have never once respected a supply contract. See the Risk factors section. Read it twice.

Thank you for your trust, your attendance, and your bananas.

The Chief Executive Officer
Guild Master, Booty Bay Banana Company · Booty Bay, Stranglethorn Vale

Commentary

Demand for Stranglethorn Standard bananas remains structurally inelastic. Hunter pets eat regardless of price, and the raid-night feast market shows no sign of substituting toward fish. We expect the spot price to appreciate steadily through the year, with seasonal softness in the second quarter as the desk rotates onto alternate characters and consumption drops.

Premium Tel'Abim product continues to command a roughly three-times multiple over Standard. We have no plans to compete on price in that segment; we plan to compete on being the only people who know where Tel'Abim is.

On the supply side, the Vale remains the Vale. Monsoon-season latency, troll activity around the Gurubashi Arena, and the periodic appearance of a raid boss in the middle of our best plantation are all modeled in the lower bound of the forecast range.

Strategic priorities

PRIORITY 1

Scale the trading desk to 40 seats

Recruit nine net new traders across liquidity, yield, and risk, with a bench of six to cover absence. Target: full desk by end of Q1, bench by end of Q2.

PRIORITY 2

Clear every tier on the calendar

Each new raid tier is cleared on the highest difficulty the desk fields within the quarter it opens. No tier carries over into the next one.

PRIORITY 3

Build a six-week banana reserve

The guild bank holds six weeks of flasks, feasts, and repair gold at all times, funded by a Harvest division that no longer has to be asked.

PRIORITY 4

Professionalize Risk & Compliance

Loot policy published and versioned. Attendance tracked, not remembered. A standing-in-the-fire ledger, reviewed monthly, with names.

PRIORITY 5

Open the retail channel

A dedicated Auction House operation turning surplus harvest into treasury. Pilot in Q2; scale in Q3 if margins hold above 30 percent.

PRIORITY 6

Invest in the dock

Social events, off-nights, and alt content so that the Company is somewhere people want to be on days the desk is closed.

Capital allocation

The Company intends to allocate treasury in fiscal 2027 in the following order of priority: first, the banana reserve, until coverage reaches six weeks; second, consumables and repairs for the trading desk; third, the retail pilot; fourth, social and dock investments. The Company does not pay a dividend. The Company has never paid a dividend. If you were told the Company pays a dividend, please report the goblin who told you.

Risk factors

An investment in, employment by, or trial membership with the Company involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the following before showing up on Tuesday.

  1. Supply risk. Our harvest operations are concentrated in a single jungle. That jungle contains trolls, tigers, raptors, panthers, an arena that spawns a riot every three hours, and at least one named boss who considers our plantation his. A material disruption to the Vale would materially disrupt the Company.
  2. Hunter pet consumption. A portion of inventory is lost each quarter to pets owned by members of the trading desk. We have raised this with the owners. The owners have raised it with the pets. The pets have no comment.
  3. Regulatory risk. Our operating environment is subject to change without notice by an authority in Irvine, California. A single patch note can reprice every instrument on our board. We have no lobbying budget and would not know who to send it to.
  4. Key-person risk. The desk is materially dependent on a small number of officers. If the Chief Operating Officer's cat sits on the keyboard during a pull, the pull is lost. We have asked for a second cat as a hedge and been refused.
  5. Headcount risk. Our guidance assumes forty people with free evenings. People acquire children, jobs, and partners who want them to do something else on Thursdays. We have no derivative that hedges this.
  6. Currency risk. Gold is subject to inflation at a rate the Company does not control and does not fully understand. Bananas are not, which is the whole thesis.
  7. Infrastructure risk. Our logistics function relies on a server, a Discord, and several internet connections located in homes with varying numbers of microwaves. Latency during the monsoon season has historically been severe.
  8. Competitive risk. The Venture Company continues to operate in the region with a lower cost of capital and no ethics. Gadgetzan interests have expressed interest in "water futures," which we do not believe is a real market but are monitoring.
  9. Standing in the fire. See Value 04. Despite extensive training, the desk continues to stand in it. This is the single largest controllable expense on the income statement.
